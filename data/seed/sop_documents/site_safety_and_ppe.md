## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### Minimum PPE for All Valve Operations

The following PPE is mandatory for any field crew member operating valves or
working at a pipe isolation site:

- **Safety helmet** (hard hat) — protects against falling objects and bumps in
  confined or underground spaces
- **Safety gloves** — protects hands from contaminated water, sharp edges on
  valve components, and provides grip on wet handwheels
- **Safety shoes** (steel-toed) — protects feet from heavy valve keys, dropped
  tools, and provides grip on wet or muddy ground

### Additional PPE for Specific Situations

| Situation | Additional PPE |
|-----------|---------------|
| Working near traffic | High-visibility vest |
| Excavation work | Full-length trousers, eye protection |
| Using power tools | Ear protection, eye protection |
| Confined space entry | Full harness, gas detector, breathing apparatus |

## Excavation Safety

### Flooded Excavation Procedure

If an excavation (trench or pit dug to access buried pipes or valves) is filling
with water:

1. **Deploy a submersible pump** to remove the water first.
2. Monitor the water level — it should be dropping steadily.
3. If the water level **does not drop** with one pump running:
   - Deploy a **second pump**.
4. If the water level **still does not drop** with two pumps running:
   - **Stop all work immediately.**
   - **Inform your supervisor.** There is likely an active water source feeding
     the excavation (an unclosed valve, a burst pipe, or groundwater ingress)
     that cannot be overcome by pumping alone.
5. **Never enter a flooded excavation** to work on pipes or valves. The water
   may be contaminated, the trench walls may be unstable when saturated, and
   submerged hazards (sharp pipe ends, exposed live services) are invisible.

### Before Entering Any Excavation

Even when dry, check:
- Trench walls are properly shored or battered to safe angles
- No overhead services (electrical cables) cross the trench
- A means of escape (ladder) is available at all times
- Another crew member is topside and able to assist in an emergency

## Confined Space Entry

Working inside chambers, pits, or enclosed valve boxes that are not open-air
is classified as **confined space work** and requires:

1. A valid confined space entry permit (issued by your supervisor)
2. Atmospheric testing (oxygen level, combustible gases, toxic gases)
3. A standby person stationed at the entry point at all times
4. A rescue plan and rescue equipment on site
5. Communication equipment between the entrant and the standby person

**Do not enter any enclosed space without completing all five steps above.** If
conditions change while inside (feeling dizzy, smelling gas, water level rising),
exit immediately and inform your supervisor.

## Working Near Pressurised Mains

- Always assume a pipe is pressurised until proven otherwise through the
  isolation verification procedure (check a fitting for flow).
- Never cut, drill, or loosen any fitting on a pipe that has not been confirmed
  depressurised.
- Stand to the side (not directly in front of) any fitting being loosened —
  residual pressure can cause a fitting to blow outwards.
- If water sprays when loosening a fitting, **retighten immediately** — a valve
  upstream has not been fully closed.

## Incident Reporting

Any of the following must be reported to your supervisor immediately:
- Injury to any person (however minor)
- Near-miss (a situation that could have caused injury)
- Damage to a valve, pipe, or fitting
- Unplanned water loss (a burst, a leak from an operated valve)
- Valve found in an unexpected state (closed when plan said open, or vice versa)
- Customer complaint about no water or discoloured water

Do not wait until the end of the shift to report — immediate reporting allows the
supervisor to respond while the situation can still be corrected.
