This document covers water quality management during and after pipe isolation
operations, including flushing requirements and customer communication.

---

## Why Discoloured Water Occurs After Reopening Valves

When valves are reopened after an isolation operation, residents downstream may
experience **dirty, brown, or discoloured water** for a short period.

**Cause:** Resuspension of naturally occurring mineral deposits — primarily iron
and manganese compounds — that settle in the pipe interior during normal
operation. These deposits are harmless at typical concentrations but are visually
concerning to residents.

**Triggers:**
- A sudden change in flow direction when valves are reopened
- Increased flow velocity as supply is restored
- Turbulence at valve seats and pipe joints
- Air pockets collapsing during refill

**The water is safe to drink** at typical discolouration levels, but residents
should be advised to flush before drinking for aesthetic reasons.

## Flushing Before Restoring Customer Supply

After completing an isolation operation and reopening all valves in reverse order,
the main must be flushed before supply is considered restored.

### Standard Flushing Procedure

1. Open the nearest hydrant or washout downstream of the operated valves.
2. Let water run for **10 to 15 minutes** continuously.
3. Check water clarity visually — it should be clear with no visible discolouration.
4. If a **turbidity meter** is available, measure the turbidity of the flushing
   water. The target is **below 5 NTU** (Nephelometric Turbidity Units).
5. If turbidity is below 5 NTU after 10–15 minutes of flushing, the main is
   considered clean and customer supply can be restored.

### Extended Flushing

If turbidity remains **above 5 NTU** after the standard 15-minute flush:
- Continue flushing for **20 to 30 minutes** total.
- Recheck turbidity after the extended period.
- If turbidity is still not below 5 NTU after 30 minutes, inform your supervisor
  — the pipe section may need a more intensive cleaning operation scheduled
  separately.

### Equipment Required

- Turbidity meter (handheld, field-rated)
- Hydrant key
- Timer
- Drainage provisions (the flush water goes to stormwater, not back into supply)

## Advising Residents About Discoloured Water

If residents report discoloured water after your operation:

1. **Advise them to flush their taps for 10 to 15 minutes.** This clears the
   stagnant discoloured water from the service pipe between the main and their
   property.
2. **Reassure them the water is safe** — the discolouration is mineral deposits,
   not contamination.
3. **If the issue persists after 15 minutes of flushing**, ask them to contact you
   again. Persistent discolouration beyond 15 minutes of flushing may indicate a
   problem requiring further investigation.

## Turbidity Measurement

**Turbidity** is a measure of water clarity. Higher values mean more suspended
particles (murkier water).

| NTU Reading | Appearance | Action |
|-------------|-----------|--------|
| < 1 NTU     | Crystal clear | Normal supply — no action needed |
| 1–5 NTU     | Slightly hazy (often invisible in a glass) | Acceptable for supply restoration |
| 5–20 NTU    | Visibly cloudy | Continue flushing — do not restore supply |
| > 20 NTU    | Heavily discoloured | Extended flush required; escalate if no improvement |

**Target for supply restoration: below 5 NTU.**

## Air Locks After Refilling a Main

When a pipe section is refilled after isolation work, air can become trapped in
high points of the pipe, creating an **air lock** that blocks water flow even
though the pipe is technically pressurised.

### Symptoms
- No water flow at a downstream point despite valves being reopened
- Sputtering or intermittent flow from taps in the affected zone
- Gurgling sounds from pipes

### Remedy
1. Check the **nearby air valves** (automatic air release valves installed at pipe
   high points). These should vent trapped air automatically as the pipe refills.
2. If the air lock persists despite the air valve being present, the air valve may
   not be functioning correctly (stuck float, blocked orifice).
3. **Schedule a maintenance check on the air valves** for a later date. Do not
   attempt to dismantle or repair an air valve in the field during an isolation
   operation — it is a separate maintenance task.
4. If the air lock is preventing supply restoration to customers, inform your
   supervisor — a manual venting operation may be arranged.

### Prevention
- Refill pipes **slowly** by opening the upstream valve gradually (quarter-turn
  increments over several minutes) rather than opening fully in one motion.
- Slow refill gives air time to travel to the air valves and vent, rather than
  becoming trapped in pockets throughout the pipe.
